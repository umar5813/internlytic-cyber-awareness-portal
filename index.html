Module 1: Phishing & Social Engineering

Phishing attacks manipulate human emotions—such as fear, urgency, or curiosity—to trick victims into revealing passwords, OTPs, or financial information.

Phishing Red Flags Example

From: alerts@bank-security-update99.com
Subject: FINAL WARNING: Account Suspended!
"Dear Customer, unusual login detected. Verify your ATM PIN and password within 1 hour or lose access permanently."

Why it's fake: Generic greeting ("Dear Customer"), fake domain, extreme urgency, and asking for PIN/password.

Safe Security Habit
  • Never click login links inside urgent emails or SMS messages.
  • Open a new browser tab and type the official website address yourself.
  • Verify sender email headers carefully (look past the display name).
  • Report suspicious emails to your organization's IT/SOC team immediately.

Module 2: Password Safety & Multi-Factor Authentication

Weak or reused passwords are the #1 cause of account takeovers via credential stuffing and brute-force attacks.

Weak Password Patterns
  • Umar1234 — Predictable name + sequential numbers (cracked in milliseconds).
  • P@ssw0rd2026 — Common dictionary substitutions are guessed instantly by automated tools.
  • Using the exact same password for Gmail, Banking, and shopping sites.
Strong Passphrase Strategy
  • Use Passphrases (14+ chars): e.g., Blue-Mango#Rocket92!Sky
  • Enable MFA: Even if a password leaks, Authenticator App (TOTP) or FIDO2 keys block attackers.
  • Use a Password Manager: Generate unique random passwords for every account.

Module 3: Anatomy of Suspicious Links

Attackers disguise malicious URLs using look-alike characters (typosquatting) and deceptive subdomains.

Deceptive URL Tricks
  • Typosquatting: www.micros0ft-login.com (zero instead of 'o').
  • Subdomain Deception: https://accounts.google.com.verify-user.xyz — The actual owner is verify-user.xyz!
  • URL Shorteners: Blindly clicking bit.ly links in unsolicited DMs.
How to Inspect Before Clicking
  • Hover your mouse over any link to preview the true destination URL in the bottom corner.
  • Read the domain right before the first single forward slash (/).
